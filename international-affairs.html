MINING & GOVERNANCE · 20 NOVEMBER 2026

Mrima Hill: proposed mineral project award raises questions about legal scrutiny

Reports dated 19 November 2026 indicate that Kenya identified Frontier Minerals Consortium as the preferred bidder for the Mrima Hill niobium and rare earth minerals project in Kwale County.

Important distinction: A notice of intention to award or preferred-bidder selection is not itself proof that a final mining contract or licence has been issued.

Potential legal and constitutional scrutiny

Public reporting describes objections from competing interests concerning aspects of the selection process. Such objections may lead to administrative or judicial review. However, a claim that the process was unconstitutional remains an allegation unless established through the relevant legal process.

Any challenge would require examination of the procurement record, applicable mining legislation, public participation requirements, environmental approvals and affected community rights.

Implications for investors

Clear procedures, regulatory certainty and meaningful engagement with local communities are essential for the financing and long-term sustainability of strategic mineral projects. Disputes may affect timing, risk assessment and investment confidence.

BL Capital perspective

BL Capital supports transparent, lawful and commercially sustainable investment in Africa’s mineral resources. Sound governance and responsible partnerships are fundamental to durable investment outcomes.

INTERNATIONAL AFFAIRS · 20 NOVEMBER 2026

US–China diplomatic engagement: implications for trade and African investment

Diplomatic engagement between the United States and China continues to influence global trade, industrial policy, technology and strategic mineral supply chains. Reports concerning a 19 November 2026 leaders' meeting have renewed attention to the economic consequences of the bilateral relationship.

Trade and critical minerals

Both countries play major roles in manufacturing, advanced technologies and mineral processing. Changes in their trade relations can influence the availability of financing, processing capacity, market access and commercial partnerships.

What this means for Africa

African economies have opportunities to attract investment into mineral processing, infrastructure, logistics and technology. Governments and businesses must also assess geopolitical exposure and ensure that agreements support local value creation.

BL Capital perspective

BL Capital advocates diversified international commercial partnerships and investment structures that strengthen Africa’s industrial capabilities and support sustainable economic growth.

AFRICAN DEVELOPMENT FINANCE · 20 NOVEMBER 2026

Mo Ibrahim urges Africa to tackle capital flight amid declining development aid

Business Insider Africa reports that British-Sudanese entrepreneur and philanthropist Mo Ibrahim has called on African governments to address capital flight as international development assistance to sub-Saharan Africa declines. The article highlights an estimated $90 billion in annual illicit financial flows from Africa, a figure drawn from earlier research rather than a newly measured 2026 total.

Why this matters

The reported reduction in bilateral assistance—approximately 26% in 2025, with a further decline of roughly 12% projected for 2026—adds pressure to national budgets and development programmes. These figures are estimates and projections, not final audited outcomes for every country.

Mobilising African capital

Reducing illicit financial flows requires effective tax administration, transparent procurement, stronger financial oversight and cooperation across borders. At the same time, institutional investors, pension funds, domestic capital markets and diaspora investment can play a larger role in financing viable African projects.

BL Capital perspective

For BL Capital Ltd, the wider lesson is the importance of well-structured, investable opportunities that connect credible institutional capital with productive projects. Project origination, investment facilitation and cross-border partnerships can support sustainable financing, provided commercial diligence and sound governance remain central.
